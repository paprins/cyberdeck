## Design Context

### Users
Primarily the device owner (technically capable) plus non-technical family members. Used in normal times for curiosity/prep, but critically in emergency / SHTF scenarios: power outages, natural disasters, grid-down situations. Device runs on battery. Users may be stressed, tired, or operating under duress. Clarity and speed of comprehension are life-critical.

### Brand Personality
Tactical. Operational. Prepared.
Not sci-fi fantasy — grounded in utility, but unapologetically bold. The aesthetic of a military operations terminal: unambiguous, readable at a glance, built for action under pressure. Orange is a *signal color*, not decoration — it means "pay attention here."

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark. The device is used in low-light emergencies, conserves battery in dark mode, and the operator context calls for a serious, command-center feel.
- **Visual tone**: Tactical operations terminal. Think: military ops room display, aircraft carrier CIC screen, emergency operations center board. Sharp corners, vivid signal orange, disciplined grid, uppercase-underscore label convention throughout.
- **References**: Military operations rooms (aircraft carrier CIC), FEMA emergency operations center displays, Starlink ground station terminals, Garmin inReach satellite communicator UI.
- **Anti-references**: Consumer SaaS dashboards (Notion, Linear), pastel health apps, glassmorphism (frosted glass cards, glow borders), playful rounded-pill buttons with gradient fills.
- **Typography**: Space Grotesk (headlines and labels — geometric, high technical legibility). Body: Inter (workhorse body copy — high x-height, readable under duress). All UI labels, titles, and navigation use UPPERCASE_UNDERSCORE convention (e.g. MEDICAL_ARCHIVE, MAP_TILES).
- **Color**: Very dark near-black background (warm-neutral, not blue-black; `--color-surface: #131313`). Vivid signal orange (`--color-primary-container: #ff6b00`) as the operational accent. `--color-primary: #ffb693` is the lighter salmon used for text/label tints — distinct from the signal orange. Tile category colors are purposeful diagnostic signals: each module category has a unique hue. Never use orange decoratively.
- **Orange rule**: Signal orange (`--color-primary-container`) appears only where attention is required — active nav state, download progress, critical status, primary CTAs. Every use has operational meaning. Overuse kills the signal.
- **Module tile pattern**: Module cards use a photo hero occupying the upper half of the card — desaturated (grayscale) at rest, full color on hover/focus. A 2px bottom-bar in `--color-primary-container` appears on hover. No transition; the snap is instant (`transition-none`). This desaturate-to-color mechanic is the only sanctioned hover animation.

### Design Principles
1. **Clarity under stress** — hierarchy must be instantly obvious, even at a glance or under duress
2. **Touch-first, never tiny** — minimum 48px touch targets, preferably much larger for primary actions
3. **Power awareness** — no animations that burn CPU/GPU, no gradients that require compositing, no blur effects
4. **Tool, not toy** — earn trust through precision and restraint; decorative elements must carry information
5. **Family-legible** — non-technical users should navigate confidently without instruction
6. **Signal, not noise** — orange is reserved for operational meaning; use it sparingly so it retains authority

### 7 Tiebreaker Principles (from original design spec)
1. Evidence over decoration — if an element doesn't carry information, remove it
2. Brand orange as signal, not noise — vivid orange only where attention is operationally required
3. Calm under pressure — layout must feel stable and scannable, never chaotic
4. Token-first — never hardcode hex/oklch values; always use CSS variables
5. Developer-reader first — technical operators can read dense information; don't over-simplify
6. WCAG 2.1 AA floor — minimum contrast for all text, critical under duress
7. Four anti-references guardrail — when in doubt, check against: consumer SaaS, pastel health apps, glassmorphism, gradient pill buttons
